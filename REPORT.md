# Heever: an AI support agent for @SpotifyCares, and how far to trust it

Hiver SDE Intern take-home. Repo: https://github.com/SirjanSingh/Heever

Every number in this file is regenerated by `make eval` from committed artifacts.

> **Read this before anything else.** No human reviewed any label in the golden set. The
> deadline ran out before the hand-labelling pass, so the reference labels are gpt-5.4's
> pre-labels used as-is, and `data/golden_200.csv` records that on every row
> (`reviewed: false`, `label_source: gpt-5.4-prelabel`). The brief asked for a hand-labelled
> set. This submission does not have one, and every number below is agreement between two
> OpenAI models rather than accuracy against ground truth. Section 5 limitation 1 is the
> full statement.

## Headline

On 100 randomly sampled SpotifyCares opening tweets, the classifier reproduces the gpt-5.4
reference labels on **56%** of rows (Wilson 46 to 65), against 39% for a tuned TF-IDF SVM
(p=0.02) and 17% for majority class. It automates **nothing**: 0% automation rate, exactly
equal to an always-escalate baseline.

The interesting part is that automating nothing is the only defensible setting here. Sweeping
the confidence threshold against this project's own cost model shows that **every threshold
which automates a single message breaches the cost ceiling on held-out data**, and the only
thresholds that respect it automate zero. Section 3 has the sweep.

## 1. Problem framing

**Brand: SpotifyCares.** Chosen on measured DM deflection, not preference. AppleSupport, the
popular pick (28 of about 110 public submissions to this assignment on GitHub), pushes 52 to 64%
of its public replies into DMs, so "ground the reply in how the brand resolved this" has nothing
to ground in. Spotify's public answers are troubleshooting steps and licensing explanations that
are still true today.

**What good means here.** A wrong auto-sent reply on a public channel costs more than a needless
escalation. The agent's job is to automate the cases where a public answer is safe (playback
fixes, how-to, catalogue questions, feature feedback) and route everything that needs an account,
money or judgement to a person, with a machine-readable reason. Good = a low bad-auto-send rate at
a useful automation rate, not high accuracy.

**Pipeline.** Redact PII and scan for injection, classify intent (gpt-5-nano, constrained to the
frozen taxonomy), retrieve three precedents (BM25 and MiniLM fused with RRF, then MMR, filtered by
brand and intent), draft a reply under a JSON schema, validate commitments against the evidence,
then a policy ladder where deterministic rules can veto automation but never authorise it.

**Not built, on purpose.** Multi-turn dialogue (scope is the opening tweet). A fine-tuned
generator. Any UI, vector database or agent framework. BLEU/ROUGE reply similarity as a metric
(it rewards "please DM us"). Any claim about resolution rate or CSAT, which offline evaluation
cannot measure.

## 2. Data, taxonomy and golden set

- Corpus: Kaggle `thoughtvector/customer-support-on-twitter`, sha256 pinned in `config.yaml`.
  1,390 SpotifyCares threads from a 5% conversation-level hash sample.
- Taxonomy: 11 in-scope intents plus `non_support` (filter) and `other_unclear` (escalate), in
  `taxonomy/intents.yaml`. Drawn by reading 160 random opening tweets; see decision log.
- Golden set: 200 opening tweets. 100 uniform random (the only headline-eligible slice), 60
  stratified by keyword to cover rare intents, 25 adversarial, 15 policy traps. Sampled by hashing
  `conversation_id`, so the draw is stable across machines.
- Labelling: **none by a human.** The design was that gpt-5.4 proposes an intent and action per
  row with a one-line rationale, and I review and correct every row, with my own confidence as
  the one field a pre-label never fills so that each row proves a human touched it. The
  labelling tool and that gating logic are both built and working
  (`scripts/build_labeling_tool.py`, `label_golden_200.html`). I ran out of time to use them.
  `scripts/run_experiment.py golden-from-prelabels` therefore writes the pre-labels straight
  through, stamping `reviewed: false`, `confidence: model` and
  `guideline_version: v2-prelabelled-NO-HUMAN-REVIEW` on all 200 rows.
- Override rate is not reported. It would be 0% by construction, since gold *is* the pre-label,
  and quoting that as if it were a measured agreement would be the most misleading number in
  the document.
- Committed: tweet ids and labels only, never tweet text.

## 3. Results vs baselines

Intent, on the 100 random rows. Fitted baselines train on the other 100, so nothing is tuned on
the test slice. The first column is agreement with the gpt-5.4 reference labels, not accuracy.

| System | Agreement (95% CI) | Macro-F1 (95% CI) | McNemar p vs Heever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Majority class | 0.17 (0.11 to 0.26) | 0.02 (0.02 to 0.03) | <0.001 |
| Random by prior | 0.11 (0.06 to 0.19) | 0.07 (0.03 to 0.11) | <0.001 |
| Keyword rules | 0.25 (0.18 to 0.34) | 0.16 (0.10 to 0.22) | <0.001 |
| TF-IDF word+char, LinearSVC, tuned | 0.39 (0.30 to 0.49) | 0.33 (0.22 to 0.43) | 0.020 |
| MiniLM nearest labelled neighbour | 0.35 (0.26 to 0.45) | 0.29 (0.20 to 0.36) | 0.003 |
| **Heever (gpt-5-nano, constrained)** | **0.56 (0.46 to 0.65)** | **0.44 (0.32 to 0.53)** | |

Heever beats every baseline and the gap over the strongest fitted one is significant, though
0.56 against 0.39 with 100 rows is a 17-point edge with overlapping intervals. The keyword rules
fire on only 18% of messages, so most of their 0.25 is the fallback class. Out-of-scope
detection is the worst result in the table and does not appear in it: precision, recall and F1
are all **0.00** on the 5 `other_unclear` rows, so the system never once identified a message it
could not classify.

**Auto-handle vs escalate, same 100 rows.** The system escalated all 100. Against the reference
labels that is 32% correct, with 0 bad auto-sends and 68 needless escalations. The
always-escalate baseline scores 0.32 (0.24 to 0.42) on the same rows, which is the same number,
because in this configuration Heever *is* the always-escalate baseline.

**The threshold sweep, which is the real finding.** `tune` picks tau on the non-random strata
and reports on the random 100, so the headline slice never chooses anything. Elkan's ceiling at
the stated 20:1 cost ratio allows bad auto-sends to be at most 4.76% of whatever is automated.

| tau | Automated (tuning slice) | Bad share | Automated (held-out random) | Bad share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 42 | 0.024 ok | 40 | **0.100 breach** |
| 0.45 | 31 | 0.032 ok | 32 | **0.125 breach** |
| 0.55 | 28 | 0.036 ok | 32 | **0.125 breach** |
| 0.60 | 18 | 0.056 breach | 20 | **0.150 breach** |
| 0.70 | 5 | 0.000 ok | 7 | **0.143 breach** |
| 0.75 and above | 0 | n/a | 0 | n/a |

Tuning chose 0.30, where the tuning slice shows a safe 2.4%. On held-out data that same
threshold produces 10%, more than double the ceiling. Read down the right-hand columns: **every
threshold that automates even one message breaches the cost constraint on held-out data, and
every threshold that respects it automates nothing.** The tuning slice was fooled because its
"safe" readings rest on a single bad auto-send out of 42. So the 0% automation rate this system
shipped with is correct, but it was correct by accident: the gate was unreachable (failure mode
4) rather than calibrated.

Reply quality on the 100 random rows. The same judge (gpt-5.4-mini, binary rubric, both criterion
orders, a tie counts as fail) scores three replies per message against the same retrieved
precedents and the brand's measured style card. Counts out of 100; p is paired McNemar against
Heever. Source: `artifacts/results/judge_summary.json`.

| Criterion | Heever draft | Brand's historical reply | Constant "Please DM us" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Addresses the stated problem | **75** | 28 (p<0.001) | 17 (p<0.001) |
| Grounded in retrieved precedents | 21 | 21 (p=0.85) | 90 (p<0.001) |
| No unsupported commitment | 75 | 91 (p=0.01) | 100 (p<0.001) |
| Brand voice | 37 | 64 (p<0.001) | 67 (p<0.001) |
| Right auto vs escalate call | 83 | 75 (p=0.20) | 73 (p=0.09) |
| No PII or unsafe content | 93 | 86 (p=0.17) | 100 (p=0.02) |
| **All six** | 14 (Wilson 9 to 22) | 8 (p=0.24) | 12 (p=0.82) |

What this says: the drafts engage with the actual problem far more often than either
alternative, and are worse on voice and on staying inside what the precedents support. The
all-six composite cannot separate a real draft from a constant string (14 vs 12), so it is not
used as a headline. Per-criterion order flips: 6 to 30 of 100 for Heever (brand voice worst).

**Judge vs human agreement: not measured.** The brief asks for it and this submission does not
have it, for the same reason the golden set has no human labels. The machinery is built and
tested: `make judge-tool` generates a blind scoring page over the same 30 drafts
(`scripts/build_judge_tool.py`, verified to embed no judge verdict and no system label, so a
scorer cannot be anchored), and `judge-import` attaches the answers and computes per-criterion
kappa against the kappa-intra ceiling. It has never been run against a human. So every judge
number in the table above is unvalidated: I can show the judge is self-inconsistent under
criterion reordering, but I cannot show whether it agrees with a person.

## 4. Top 5 failure modes

Modes 1 to 3 are measured over all 197 drafts and need no labels. Modes 4 and 5 are measured
against the gpt-5.4 reference labels on the random 100, so read them with limitation 1 in mind.

1. **Drafts don't sound like SpotifyCares.** Mean draft length 204 characters against the brand's
   130. Sign-off initials ("/TB") in 2% of drafts against 73% of brand replies. A question in 15%
   against 48%. The judge agrees: brand voice 37/100 against 64 for history. *Hypothesis:* the
   style card is passed as JSON statistics, which the model reads as metadata rather than as a
   target. *Test:* replace it with five verbatim brand replies and re-measure length and sign-off
   rate on the same 100 rows.
2. **The prompt's vocabulary leaks into public replies.** 8 drafts talk about "policy", e.g. g006:
   "We don't have a policy-based reply here, but you can try...". Others say "flagged this for a
   quick DM handoff" (g005) or ask "Would you like me to escalate this?" (g048). A customer
   should never see the system's internal words. *Hypothesis:* the empty playbook block reads
   "(no playbook for this intent)" and the model narrates it. *Test:* drop the empty block and
   grep the same terms.
3. **The code grounding validator passes what the judge fails.** g001 to g006 all passed
   `grounding.validate_draft` and failed the judge's grounding criterion. The validator only
   checks typed commitments (refund amounts, timelines); invented troubleshooting steps and
   assurances like "get you back in ASAP" (g005) sail through. *Test:* hand-check 20 validator
   passes for any unsupported claim.

4. **The confidence gate sits above the model's entire output range, so the agent can never
   automate anything.** `tau_intent` is 0.75. The highest confidence gpt-5-nano emitted across all
   200 messages is 0.78, and both rows that reach it are `account_access`, whose playbook escalates
   anyway. Across the five intents that can auto-send, the ceiling is 0.74. 96 of the 200
   messages carry one of those five intents and not one cleared the bar, so 184 die at rung 4
   and the 0% automation rate is arithmetic, not policy. The scores are not probabilities
   either: 200 messages produce 29 distinct values, four of which (0.62, 0.58, 0.42, 0.72)
   cover 124 rows. And 0.75 was already far more permissive than the cost model justifies,
   since Elkan puts break-even at 0.9524 for R = 20, so no threshold on this score is both
   cost-justified and reachable. *Hypothesis:* a confidence field a model fills in its own JSON
   output is a stylistic habit, not a posterior. The gap is calibration, not tuning. The sweep
   in section 3 is the test, and it agrees.

5. **The system never abstains, and its errors sit on taxonomy seams.** `other_unclear` is the
   abstain class, the one that routes a message nobody can confidently classify to a human. On
   the random 100 it scores precision, recall and F1 of 0.00 across all 5 of its rows: the
   classifier gave every one a confident in-scope intent instead, usually `dm_followup`. A
   classifier that cannot say "I do not know" is the wrong shape for a system whose whole value
   is knowing when to escalate. The rest of the errors concentrate on adjacent pairs rather than
   scattering: `subscription_plan` read as `billing_payment` (5), `feature_feedback` as
   `playback_technical` (4), `non_support` as `feature_feedback` (3), `complaint_escalation` as
   `dm_followup` (3). `feature_feedback` alone is misread 8 times across three classes.
   *Hypothesis:* those pairs are genuinely adjacent in the taxonomy I wrote, since "my plan costs
   too much" is both a subscription and a billing sentence, so part of this is my class
   boundaries rather than the model. *Test:* two labellers independently on the 40 rows along
   those seams, checking whether they agree with each other more than the model agrees with
   either. Needs a human; not run. These are disagreements with gpt-5.4, not with truth.

## 5. What is misleading about my headline number

1. **There is no human label anywhere in this evaluation.** Every reference label is gpt-5.4's,
   so the headline measures whether gpt-5-nano reproduces gpt-5.4. Both are OpenAI models that
   plausibly share training data and failure modes, so their agreement is an upper bound on
   agreement with a person, and a loose one. It is also not evidence of correctness: where
   gpt-5.4 is wrong in a way gpt-5-nano copies, the row scores as a success. This is the largest
   defect in the submission and it invalidates every number here as a measure of quality. What
   survives is relative ranking under a fixed reference, which is why the baselines table is the
   part worth reading and the headline is not.
2. **I wrote the taxonomy and the labels were produced against it.** Ambiguous cases were settled
   by a definition written by the person who benefits from them being settleable, then applied by
   a model prompted with that definition. Failure mode 5 shows the errors landing exactly on the
   seams I drew.
3. **Zero annotators, so there is no ceiling for anything.** The protocol calls for one annotator
   plus a blind re-label after 48 hours, giving an intra-annotator kappa that bounds how well any
   judge could possibly do. With no annotator there is no ceiling, no judge validation, and no
   way to tell a judge that disagrees with people from one that is right.
4. **Judge and generator share a vendor.** Both are OpenAI models (gpt-5.4-mini judges gpt-5-nano)
   because only one API key was available in time. Self-preference bias is the best documented
   judge bias.
5. **n = 100.** The headline CI is about plus or minus 8 to 10 points. Per-class numbers on the
   rare intents rest on a handful of rows each.
6. **Survivorship.** The corpus only contains tweets Spotify chose to answer publicly. Ignored
   complaints and DM-only contacts are missing, and they are the harder cases.
7. **"DM us" wins on similarity.** Which is why no similarity metric is reported and the constant
   reply sits in the quality table as a control.
8. **Offline proxy only.** No resolution, CSAT or repeat-contact data. Every number is an upper
   bound on a proxy.
9. **Contamination.** The dataset is public since 2017 and may be in the models' training data.
10. **Frozen 2017 snapshot.** Plans, prices and features have changed; a reply that matched
    history can be wrong today.
11. **The judge is unstable.** Scoring the same draft with the criteria in reverse order
    changes the verdict on 6 to 30 of 100 drafts depending on the criterion. Ties count as fail,
    so every pass rate above is a lower bound under one prompt wording.
12. **A composite that a constant string ties is not evidence.** All-six-pass is 14 for real
    drafts and 12 for "Sorry to hear that! Please DM us". Only per-criterion numbers are quoted.
13. **One threshold was tuned, on the non-random strata only.** `tau_intent` is the only tuned
    parameter. `scripts/run_experiment.py tune` sweeps 0.30 to 0.95 over the 100 non-random rows
    and never reads the random 100, so the headline slice did not choose it. Everything else
    (`tau_retrieval` 0.35, the 20:1 cost ratio, `auto_refund_ceiling_usd` 50,
    `max_thread_turns_auto` 4) was fixed before the first run and left alone. The honest caveat
    is that the tuning worked and the result still did not generalise: the chosen 0.30 breaches
    the cost ceiling on held-out data by more than double, because the tuning slice's safety
    margin rested on a single bad auto-send out of 42.
14. **Three filtered messages are counted as escalations.** `non_support` is a filter class:
    the pipeline drops it before the policy ladder and never drafts a reply. But `Action` is a
    closed four-value enum with no `FILTER` member, deliberately, because the injection defence
    depends on that space staying narrow, so the drop is recorded as `escalate` with a reason
    saying it was filtered (`docs/17 §3`). Three of the 200 rows are these. Every escalation count
    here is therefore three too high, and the automation-rate denominator includes three messages
    the system is designed never to answer. The `evidence_ids: ["filtered"]` marker that
    distinguishes them survives in `decisions.jsonl` but is dropped from `predictions.jsonl`,
    which is the file the evaluation actually reads, so within Tier 1 they are separable only by
    `intent == "non_support"`.

## 6. With one more week

In priority order, and the first item is worth more than the rest combined.

- **Label the golden set by hand**, then a second annotator on the random 100 and the 48-hour
  blind re-label, so there is an inter- and intra-annotator ceiling to compare anything against.
  Without this every number in section 3 stays a model-agreement number. The tool is built; this
  is roughly two hours of work that the deadline ate.
- **Score the 30 judge drafts by hand** so the judge has a validated agreement rate. Another
  fifteen minutes, and it closes the brief's judge-agreement requirement.
- **Replace self-reported confidence with something calibrated.** The sweep in section 3 shows
  the current score cannot support any safe operating point. The cheap version is logprob-based
  or an ensemble over paraphrases; the honest version is a small fitted calibrator on held-out
  labelled data, which again needs labels.
- A different-family judge (Gemini or an open model on the DGX) and the 3-paraphrase flip rate,
  to break the shared-vendor bias in limitation 4.
- Build the precedent index from the full corpus instead of a 5% sample (176 precedents today).
- Per-intent playbooks distilled from resolved threads, each line carrying evidence ids, which
  should also fix the prompt-vocabulary leakage in failure mode 2.

## Decision log

1. **SpotifyCares, not AppleSupport**, on measured DM deflection. A brand that answers in DMs
   leaves nothing public to ground a reply in.
2. **Rewrote the taxonomy after reading real tweets.** The draft's cross-industry classes
   (order status, returns, delivery) do not exist for a streaming service.
3. **Added `dm_followup`.** "I DM'd you" carries no problem statement and only exists on public
   channels. No industry taxonomy has it; it showed up in the first 160 tweets.
4. **Split playback failures from lost libraries.** Same symptom in the tweet, different fix: one
   is generic troubleshooting, the other needs the account.
5. **Pre-labelled with gpt-5.4, not the classifier's model.** Pre-labels anchor the annotator; if
   they came from the model under test, every accepted label would be agreement the model put
   there itself.
6. **Confidence was designed as the one field a pre-label never fills**, so a row could only
   enter the golden set once a human had deliberately touched it. The gate worked, and it is
   the reason this report can state its own defect precisely: when the hand-labelling pass did
   not happen, `cmd_golden` admitted zero rows rather than quietly passing model labels off as
   reviewed. Loading them required a second, explicitly named path
   (`golden-from-prelabels`) that stamps `reviewed: false` and
   `guideline_version: v2-prelabelled-NO-HUMAN-REVIEW` on all 200 rows.
7. **Held golden and pilot conversations out of the precedent index before building it.** A
   message must never retrieve its own brand reply as a precedent.
8. **Index resolutions, not first replies.** Most first replies are diagnostic questions; indexing
   them teaches the agent to ask questions forever. 191 of the pool's brand replies qualified,
   176 after removing near-duplicates.
9. **Fixed two gate bugs that only running the thing could find.** The no-precedent gate compared
    the RRF score (capped at 2/61, about 0.033) against a 0.35 threshold and escalated 191 of 200
    messages; it now reads the top precedent's cosine similarity. Separately, the threshold
    simulator re-scored rows at a lower tau without re-applying the grounding validator, which
    sits below the confidence gate in the ladder, so it credited drafts the pipeline would have
    vetoed: 4 phantom auto-sends at tau 0.62 and 7 at tau 0.55. Unit tests passed throughout both,
    because they used hand-picked scores and never ran the ladder end to end.
10. **The judge sees what the generator saw.** The first judge run passed no evidence. Grounding
    then failed 92 of 93 drafts ("no retrieved evidence provided"), brand voice flipped with
    criterion order on 36 of 93, and the constant "DM us" reply scored as well as real drafts.
    Every system is now judged against the same retrieved precedents, the measured style card
    and the proposed handling. The broken run is kept in
    `artifacts/results/judge_verdicts_run1_no_evidence.jsonl`.
11. **Stray model output is cleaned in code, not repaired by another call.** 47 of 73
    validation failures were an `escalation_reason` on a non-escalate action. Dropping it cut
    the repair rate from 29% to 6%; each drop is logged.
12. **`dm_handoff` counts as escalate** when scoring against the human's binary label: the public
    tweet is automated but a person does the work.
13. **Fitted baselines train on the non-random strata and test on the random 100**, so no baseline
    sees the headline slice.
14. **Judge moved from Gemini to OpenAI** under time pressure. Stated as limitation 4 rather than
    hidden.
15. **Commit tweet ids and labels, never tweet text**, following TweetSumm (EMNLP Findings 2021).

- **Corpus:** Kaggle `thoughtvector/customer-support-on-twitter`, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- **TweetSumm** (Findings of EMNLP 2021): the redistribution pattern, ids plus labels with the

- **Reciprocal Rank Fusion** (Cormack et al., SIGIR 2009): fusing the BM25 and MiniLM arms.
  MMR for diversity after fusion.
- **Elkan** (IJCAI 2001): the cost-sensitive operating point. At R = 20, auto-send only above
  `R/(1+R)` = 0.9524, equivalently hold bad auto-sends to `1/(1+R)` = 4.76% of what is
  automated. `tune` uses the second form as its ceiling.
- **Statistical significance in NLP** (ACL 2018): Wilson intervals on every proportion, McNemar
  on every paired comparison. Agreement banded against **Krippendorff's** thresholds, which are
  stricter than Landis and Koch.
- **Zheng et al.** (arXiv:2306.05685, NeurIPS 2023): LLM-as-judge position bias and
  self-enhancement, behind the order-flip protocol and limitations 4 and 11.
- **Casanueva et al. 2020** (`docs/01 §8`): published intent-classification numbers, as context
  only. Not comparable to anything here.
